# Design Direction — Website Tips Story (Day 2)

BRAND VISUAL STATUS: FLEXIBLE — FINAL IDENTITY PENDING
(`brand/visual-identity.md`)

**Revision note (Owner Review):** the prior version prescribed a plain
solid-background, centered-text layout as if that were the final
professional design. It is not — it was a draft/reference layout only.
This revision opens the treatment up for a genuine editorial/typographic
pass rather than locking in a placeholder-style card as final.

## Visual Direction
- A professional visual pass should aim for intentional Persian
  typography, clear hierarchy, strong whitespace, thoughtful alignment,
  and a restrained, human-designed composition — not decorative AI
  flourishes (no random 3D objects, glow/neon effects, meaningless
  gradients, or clip-art icons).
- Subtle interface/editorial cues (e.g. a thin rule, a small label, a
  considered asymmetric layout) may be used where they add clarity, not
  as ornament.
- No logo, no current brand color treated as permanent — background/
  accent colors remain open until final brand identity is approved; a
  dark neutral tone is one acceptable direction, not the only one.
- Typography: modern, highly readable Persian-capable typeface,
  consistent across all 3 frames for sequence cohesion.

## Layout
Full-bleed 9:16 (1080×1920). Text hierarchy should clearly separate the
primary line from the secondary line (size, weight, or placement — not
necessarily just size, as in the previous draft). Frame 3 reserves space
for the native Question Box sticker Instagram adds at posting time —
exact placement flexible as long as the sticker has clear room.

## Text Placement
Flexible — centered is acceptable but not mandatory; an intentionally
composed asymmetric layout is equally acceptable if it improves hierarchy
and visual interest.

## What Must NOT Appear
- Any specific brand color/logo treated as final
- Stock imagery
- Generic AI decoration (3D renders, glow/neon, meaningless gradients,
  random icons)
- Overcrowded text (kept to 2 short lines per frame)
- A plain centered-text card presented as though it were already the
  finished, publication-ready design
